# Modifications change the future

Apply when scope or price changes after inception: add-ons, upsells, downgrades, early
renewals, extensions, concessions, change orders. Also when a number changes without the
contract changing, to tell the two apart.

A modification is accounted for as a separate contract when it adds distinct goods or
services at their standalone selling price. Otherwise, if the remaining goods or services
are distinct from those already delivered, the old contract is treated as ended and a new
one begins, prospectively. If they are not distinct (a partly finished single
performance obligation), the change is a cumulative catch-up. A change in an estimate
with no change in the contract is never a modification. It is a catch-up.

**Why.** Revenue already recognized reflected what was delivered at the price then
agreed. A new price for future service should not rewrite the past. That is why the
common SaaS case is prospective. A single project whose scope grows is different: the
measure of progress itself changed, so the catch-up restates progress to date.

**Pattern.**
- First, did the parties change enforceable rights (a signed amendment, an approved change
  order, a practice they both follow)? No change means a re-estimate. Go to
  price-is-what-you-expect-to-keep. A signed change order is a modification even when its
  main effect is to trigger a variable term, such as pushing hours over a rebate
  threshold. Treat it as one, and say whether the remaining work is distinct (prospective)
  or part of one partly done obligation (cumulative). Services billed by the hour can be
  either, and the memo names the fact that decides.
- Added goods or services: are they distinct, and is their price at standalone selling
  price adjusted for the circumstances (an existing customer's lower selling cost counts)?
  Both yes: separate contract. Compare against the SSP range in the stream policy. A
  one-off concession is not an adjustment for circumstances.
- Otherwise, take the unrecognized consideration from the old contract, add the new
  consideration, and allocate it across what remains by relative SSP at the modification
  date (`method: prospective`). A pending point-in-time item (undelivered training) is
  part of what remains.
- Partly satisfied single obligation (a fixed-fee project): recompute progress with the
  new total, and book the difference now (`method: cumulative`).
- Downgrades and partial terminations are modifications too. A refund or credit for
  service already delivered is a price concession on the past. Assess it as variable
  consideration, not as a prospective change.

**Deciding facts.** The amendment's price per unit against the SSP range. Whether the
concession is a one-off or policy. What remains undelivered at the modification date.

**The tell.** An add-on booked on its own at the amendment price while the old schedule
runs on unchanged, with no SSP comparison.

**Codification.** 606-10-25-10 to 25-13.
